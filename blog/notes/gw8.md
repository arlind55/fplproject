## captain
<strong>Fernandes</strong> leads by 0.4, and it is worth being clear about why, because the form says the opposite. He scored 23 points in GW2 and has managed 1 or 2 in each of the five games since, and 177,000 managers have sold him this week. The model now splits penalties out of xG, so his 6.4 breaks down cleanly: 1.2 from open-play goals, 0.6 from penalties, 1.4 from assists, and the rest from minutes and bonus. That's also why he sits ahead of <strong>Mbeumo</strong> (5.4), who has the bigger open-play goal threat (2.1) but is second in line for penalties and creates less. Add a home game against Bournemouth in the match with the most expected goals of the week (United's 1.89 is the highest of any side) and he comes out on top. The model picked him once already, in GW6, and he scored 1. Treat this as its highest-conviction call, not a safe one.

<strong>Haaland</strong> is the captain most people will choose, and he carries a 75% flag. His 4.4 already includes the one-in-four chance he doesn't play. Fit, he projects at roughly 5.9 at Villa Park, still below Fernandes but close enough that captaining him to protect your rank is reasonable if he's named in the XI. If he isn't fit, <strong>Saka</strong> (6.0 at home to Everton, and Arsenal's penalty taker) is the clean alternative. <strong>Gabriel</strong> at 5.8 is the defender's version of the same Arsenal fixture: the highest clean-sheet odds of the week, and a 1-in-10 ceiling of about 9 points.

## fixtures
Arsenal at home to Everton is the standout: 44% clean-sheet odds, the only side above 35%, and 1.84 expected goals. United (1.89 xG) and Chelsea (1.83 at home to Spurs) have the best attacking fixtures. The model rates Liverpool v Brighton as close to a coin toss, and it rates City as the stronger side at Villa Park by some distance. That 34% clean-sheet chance for City is the second best of the week. Forest are the model's pick of the away sides. They have 1.78 expected goals at Ipswich on Friday night, more than City at Villa Park.

## xi
The optimiser's squad leans hard on two fixtures: three Arsenal players for the Everton game and two United midfielders for Bournemouth. It has no Haaland, and that's the price of £15.7m for a doubtful player, not a judgement on his ability. The rest of the XI has two Brighton players and one each from City, Bournemouth, Brentford and Leeds, mostly mid-priced players with secure minutes. If you're on a wildcard, start here and swap in the template players you want to keep.

## targets
The value picks (the <span class="tag">value</span> tag) are the best projection per pound among players already in the top 20 at their position, so they aren't just cheap. <strong>Thomas</strong> of Coventry is the clearest case. He has played every minute bar 13 this season and projects 18.4 points over five weeks at £4.0m, more per pound than anyone in the game. <strong>Rudoni</strong> at £4.9m is the midfield version, on penalties for Coventry, though he's started six of seven. Arsenal's next five are the easiest in the league by the model's ratings, alongside City's. That's why Gabriel and Calafiori sit at the top of the defenders despite their prices.

## crowd
Two disagreements stand out. <strong>Cherki</strong> is the second most-bought player of the week, but the model ranks him 36th among midfielders, because it gives him only a 65% chance of starting. He has started five of City's seven games, and Guardiola rotates. On the other side, <strong>Wissa</strong> is being sold after one goal from 2.97 xG. That's the profile of a forward due a goal, not one to drop, and the model has him ninth among forwards for the next five. Fernandes is the third, covered above. The crowd agrees with the model on Groß, Tavernier, De Cuyper and Rogers.

## flags
Haaland's 75% is the flag that matters: 77% of managers own him. City's pre-match press conference is the one to watch. Szoboszlai and Semenyo have no return date, so the transfers out are right. Both are 0 in the model until FPL changes their status.

## luck
Understat's expected points turn each game's chances into the points a team "should" have taken. <strong>Forest</strong> (5 points from 12.6 expected) and <strong>Bournemouth</strong> (6 from 12.3) are the two sides the table is most unfair to. By chance quality, they're the fourth and fifth best teams in the league, not 18th and 15th. Forest's next five are hard (the joint-worst run on the model's ratings). Bournemouth's are the third easiest, which is why Tavernier is the buy rather than a Forest attacker. At the other end, <strong>Chelsea</strong> are fourth in the table but 12th by expected points, and City's 21 points from 14.5 expected suggests their title-race lead is partly finishing. Neither is a reason to sell Palmer or Haaland, but it is a reason not to pay extra for Chelsea's defenders.

## record
Two weeks of live tracking is a small sample, so read this as an early check rather than a verdict. The model's average error per player has been lower than FPL's own expected-points figure in both weeks, about a third of a point better. Its ranking of players has been better on average too, though FPL's was slightly better in GW6. Its captains have scored 1 (Fernandes, GW6) and 13 (Haaland, GW7). The full week-by-week table is on the <a href="../accuracy.html">track record page</a>.

## verdict
Captain Fernandes if you own him, Haaland if he's named and you don't. Bournemouth's Tavernier is the transfer, and Coventry's Thomas is the bench. Don't follow the crowd into Cherki at a 65% start chance. Check Friday's press conferences for Haaland and Gibbs-White before the 18:30 deadline.
