The first stretch: what's been good, what's been bad, and what's changed

There's no Premier League football until GW11, so it's a good moment to step back. We compared the first ten gameweeks with the same ten from each of the last two seasons, using FPL's own match data, and with six seasons of Understat shot data. Some of what we found is the usual early-season noise. Some of it looks like a real change in how the league is playing, and that matters for how you set up your squad for the next run of fixtures.

The short version.
  • Home advantage has shrunk. Home sides have won 42% of games, down from 51% at this stage last season. Draws are up to 27%.
  • Set pieces and penalties have dried up. 15% of goals have come from set pieces (22% last season) and penalties are at their lowest rate in six seasons.
  • Cheap defenders are still the best value in the game, for the second season running since defensive contribution points arrived. 12 of the top 20 scorers are defenders or goalkeepers.
  • Haaland is reliable, not explosive. 70 points, 28 fewer than at this stage last season, and he hasn't been the top scorer in any single gameweek.
  • Forest, Bournemouth and Sunderland are better than the table says. Palace, Newcastle, Villa and Chelsea are worse.

How the game has changed

Home wins are down, but home teams aren't worse. Home sides have won 42 of the 100 games (42%), against 51 at the same point last season and a 44% average over the last 12 full seasons. The interesting part is that home teams still create 54% of the xG and score 56% of the goals, close to last season's 55% and 58%. What's changed is that close games are ending level. There have been 27 draws, against 21 last season. For FPL, this means you shouldn't lean too hard on "at home" when you pick between two similar players. The fixture's quality matters far more than the venue.

Set pieces have gone quiet. Last season was the set-piece season. 22% of goals in the first 70 games came from corners, free kicks and other set plays, and one in five goals was a header. This season it's 15%, the lowest of the six seasons we looked at, with 0.27 corner goals a game against 0.44. Headed goals are down from 21% to 13%. Defenders have still scored about as often (32 goals in their starts, 31 last season), but fewer of the league's goals now come from the moments a centre-back is most likely to score.

Penalties are rare. There have been 0.16 penalties per game in Understat's data, the lowest in six seasons and well down on last season's 0.27. That's why the model treats penalties as a separate piece of a player's projection rather than folding them into his xG. A penalty taker on a team that isn't winning any isn't worth much extra.

More shots, worse finishing. Teams are shooting more (27.6 shots a game, against 23.4 at this stage last season) and from further out, with 35% of shots from outside the box. FPL's xG is up from 2.66 to 2.95 a game, but goals have barely moved, from 2.68 to 2.73. The league as a whole has scored about 22 goals fewer than its chances were worth. Our own research on finishing says that kind of gap doesn't get "paid back", but scoring rates do drift back towards xG from here. Expect slightly more goals per game after the break than before it.

More cards. 4.0 yellows a game (3.5 last season) and 19 reds already, against 11 at this stage last season. It's a small cost per player, but it's worth checking before you bring in a midfielder who picks up a booking every other game.

Defenders still rule, for the second year

Last season introduced defensive contribution (DefCon) points: 2 points for a defender with 10 clearances, blocks, interceptions and tackles, or a midfielder or forward with 12 including recoveries. They changed the shape of the game, and this season has carried on in the same way.

The top of the points table shows it. De Cuyper (£4.5m at the start) is the highest scorer in the game with 75 points, five ahead of Haaland. Groß (£5.5m, 70), Vuskovic (£5.0m, 70) and Bogle (£4.5m, 58) are all in the top ten. Vuskovic, Brighton's third player in the top ten, is the one most managers have missed: 70 points and owned by 9%. Be aware that Brighton's defence has had some luck (see below).

Per pound, the cheapest players have paid best for two seasons now. Regular starters priced at £5.0m or less return 6.2 points per £1m. What's new is that the middle of the market has caught up. The £6.6–8.5m bracket is at 5.6, against 4.5 at this stage last season, and the £8.6–10.5m bracket has recovered from a poor start last season, thanks to Isak (61) and Palmer (47). The two players above £10.5m are the worst value in the game. Remember that defenders give you points per pound, not hauls. Per start, defenders have scored 10+ points 5.2% of the time, against 9.5% for forwards. Our own defender research found the same thing: a back line of cheap, nailed defenders gives you a high floor, but your captain and your ceiling still come from further forward.

The premium problem

Only two players started the season at £10m or more: Haaland (£15.5m) and Fernandes (£12.0m). Last season there were five. Salah is no longer in the game, and Palmer and Saka started at £9.5m.

Haaland has 70 points from 9 goals, and he's in 79% of squads. That's good, but it's 28 fewer than at this point last season (98), and he's never been the top scorer in a gameweek. His scores have been 2, 13, 9, 9, 6, 6, 13, 8, 2 and 2. Captaining him every week has made 140 points. In nine of the ten weeks, the hindsight-best captain was a player costing £8m or less, and four times it was a defender. He's been a shield more than a weapon. That doesn't make him a sell. He still has the most xG in the league by a distance (7.1), and he's the model's top captain pick for GW11, at home to Fulham. But owning him isn't giving anyone an edge.

Fernandes is the disappointment. 45 points at £12.0m, 4 goals from 3.9 xG, and he's missed a game. The model has been too keen on him. It made him captain twice in five weeks, for 1 and 0 points. His price has dropped to £11.6m and his ownership to 20%.

The good

Arsenal are top with 27 points, two more than at this stage last season, with seven clean sheets and the league's best underlying numbers (0.85 xG difference per game). Brighton are the big improvers in the table: second, with 22 points against 15 a year ago, and a goal difference of +19 that's the best in the league. City are two points better off than a year ago, despite losing 3-1 at Forest in GW10.

Among players, the cheap Brighton pair, De Cuyper and Groß (now in 46–47% of squads), have been the picks of the season. Evanilson (£6.0m, 61 points, 5 goals and 4 assists) and Tavernier (£6.0m, 58, 5 goals from 4.2 xG) have made Bournemouth's attack the best value in the game. Rogers is the most-owned midfielder (54%) with 52 points, but he's scored 5 from 3.0 xG, so some of that is finishing that won't last.

The bad

Manchester United finished third last season. They're 18th now with 8 points, against 17 at this stage last season, and haven't kept a clean sheet. GW10's 4-4 draw with Villa summed them up. Their underlying numbers have dropped, from +0.42 xG difference per game last season to zero, but that's mid-table, not relegation form. They've scored 14 from 18.0 xG. A side 18th on results and 11th on chances should pick up.

Crystal Palace are the real collapse. They're bottom, they've conceded 24 (the most in the league), and they have the worst xG difference of anyone at −0.82 a game. Last season they were slightly better than average. Newcastle have dropped almost as far (−0.54, down 0.69 on last season) and are only 12th because their finishing has held up. Sunderland had 18 points at this stage last season and have 8 now, 19th, but unlike Palace their numbers don't back that up (see below).

The worst finishers among players are Gonzalo (1 goal from 4.0 xG), Wissa (1 from 3.4) and Thiago (2 from 4.1, and now suspended until December). Barry was on that list after GW9 with 4 goals from 6.4 xG, then scored twice against Coventry for 16 points. At £5.5m he has the second-most xG in the league (7.3), behind only Haaland, and our research says players like him keep scoring at their xG rate from here on.

Luck: the table vs the chances

The chart at the top of this post compares each team's xG difference per game this season (filled dot) with last season's (hollow dot). Four teams stand out.

Nottingham Forest are 16th with 9 points, but they're fifth on xG difference (+0.46 a game), up 0.72 on last season. That's the biggest improvement in the league. They've scored 9 goals from 15.8 xG, almost 7 goals short. GW10's 3-1 win over City is what their numbers have been pointing to for weeks.

Bournemouth are 7th and third on xG difference (+0.68, up 0.51). Unlike Forest, their results have started to follow, with three wins in a row at Old Trafford, against Leeds and at Ipswich.

Sunderland are 19th, but 13th on xG difference and up 0.35 on last season. They've scored 8 from 15.7 xG, the worst finishing in the league. They're closer to a mid-table side than a relegation one, and Le Fée (£6.0m, 3.8 xG and 1.9 xA, owned by 3%) is the player to watch.

Brighton are the other way round. Their numbers are better than last season, but they've scored 26 from 19.7 xG and conceded 7 from 13.9. That's about 13 goals of luck across both ends. They're a good side, but more like a fourth-placed side than a second-placed one.

Chelsea (5th in the table, 14th on xG difference, 19 goals from 14.1 xG), Everton (6th, 12th) and Villa (10th, 15th) are the others whose results are ahead of their numbers.

The promoted sides are holding on, for now

All three promoted teams are out of the bottom three, which is United, Sunderland and Palace. Hull and Ipswich have 11 points and Coventry 9. Last season's promoted trio had 18 (Sunderland), 11 (Leeds) and 10 (Burnley) at this stage. On the underlying numbers, though, all three are in the bottom five for xG difference, and Hull have conceded 11 from 14.6 xG against. Ipswich and United are the only sides yet to keep a clean sheet. Their defenders are cheap, but the clean-sheet odds behind them are poor.

How the model has done

We've tracked the model against FPL's own expected points for five weeks, GW6 to GW10. It's had the lower average error every week: 1.96 points per player against FPL's 2.21. Its top 20 picks scored more than FPL's top 20 in all five weeks, and by the widest margin yet in GW10 (6.3 points each against 4.9). Captaincy has been the weak spot. Fernandes (1), Haaland (13), Fernandes (0), Haaland (2) and Gabriel (8) make 24 points in five weeks. The full table is on the track record page.

What to take into the break

Five things to do.
  • Keep a cheap, nailed back line. Two seasons of DefCon say it's the most reliable value in the game.
  • Don't pay extra for penalty takers or "home" fixtures. Both have been worth less this season than last.
  • Back the chances, not the goals. Barry and Forest's and Sunderland's attackers are getting the chances. Rogers and Brighton's back line have had the luck.
  • Treat Haaland as a shield. Keep him if you have him, but if you're chasing, the points have come from elsewhere.
  • Avoid Palace and Newcastle defenders. Their decline is in the chances, not just the results.

Method: FPL per-match player data for GW1–10 of 2026/27 (the site's own data), and the same gameweeks of 2025/26 and 2024/25 from the vaastav/Fantasy-Premier-League archive. xG and xG difference are FPL's figures, summed per team. Points per £m are for players with at least half the available minutes, priced at their GW1 value. Set pieces, penalties, headers and shot distance come from Understat's shot data, comparing the first 70 matches of each season, because Understat currently runs to 19 October. "Outside the box" means shots taken from more than 17% of the pitch length from goal. The numbers are generated by blog/roundup.py.
