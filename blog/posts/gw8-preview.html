Fernandes leads by 0.4, and it is worth being clear about why, because the form says the opposite. He scored 23 points in GW2 and has managed 1 or 2 in each of the five games since, and 177,000 managers have sold him this week. The model now splits penalties out of xG, so his 6.4 breaks down cleanly: 1.2 from open-play goals, 0.6 from penalties, 1.4 from assists, and the rest from minutes and bonus. That's also why he sits ahead of Mbeumo (5.4), who has the bigger open-play goal threat (2.1) but is second in line for penalties and creates less. Add a home game against Bournemouth in the match with the most expected goals of the week (United's 1.89 is the highest of any side) and he comes out on top. The model picked him once already, in GW6, and he scored 1. Treat this as its highest-conviction call, not a safe one.

Haaland is the captain most people will choose, and he carries a 75% flag. His 4.4 already includes the one-in-four chance he doesn't play. Fit, he projects at roughly 5.9 at Villa Park, still below Fernandes but close enough that captaining him to protect your rank is reasonable if he's named in the XI. If he isn't fit, Saka (6.0 at home to Everton, and Arsenal's penalty taker) is the clean alternative. Gabriel at 5.8 is the defender's version of the same Arsenal fixture: the highest clean-sheet odds of the week, and a 1-in-10 ceiling of about 9 points.

Understat's expected points turn each game's chances into the points a team "should" have taken. Forest (5 points from 12.6 expected) and Bournemouth (6 from 12.3) are the two sides the table is most unfair to. By chance quality, they're the fourth and fifth best teams in the league, not 18th and 15th. Forest's next five are hard (the joint-worst run on the model's ratings). At the other end, Chelsea are fourth in the table but 12th by expected points, and City's 21 points from 14.5 expected suggests their title-race lead is partly finishing. Neither is a reason to sell Palmer or Haaland, but it is a reason not to pay extra for Chelsea's defenders.
